### Importance of disease forecasting

The field of infectious diseases and epidemiology is one that has undergone an enormous scientific revolution. Through global endeavors in medical biology, bioinformatics and public health, circulation, morbidity and mortality of vaccine preventable diseases (VPDs) have been reduced substantially [[Shattock, 2024]]. Over fourty years ago, in 1980, humanity successfully eradicated its first disease entirely; smallpox. Although we see many challenges in further reducing morbidity caused by horrific diseases, humanity has been able to render a virus extinct that has haunted our species for centuries. The fact that we have been able to do this is nothing but intensely inspiring.

While this positive note is something that should be stressed more, not that long ago, many scientists were confident that polio and measles were next in line to be eradicated. While initial efforts were immensely effective, these diseases still persist on the planet. The eradication of polio proved more difficult, partially because of the ability to spread disease by the vaccine-strain. It also proved more difficult to vaccinate the entire population against measles. After decades of improving the situations, the disease are still around, and actually form a larger threat than experts would have thought. Vaccination rates in general, and to measles particularly, are decreasing, and for in contrary to belief, polio viruses are found in sewage water in Europe recently, suggesting that polio too is still a threat.

#### Measles
As one of the most infectious diseases that humankind has ever encountered, measles is often studied as a proxy of vaccine-preventable diseases in general. Measles may be able to highlight local gaps in immunity, more so than other, slightly less infectious, VPDs. This viral respiratory infection may be transmitted through aerosols. Among the people at risk for the disease are young children, which constitute the vast majority of cases. Measles is present globally though at very different levels geographically, and is associated with outbreaks globally.
